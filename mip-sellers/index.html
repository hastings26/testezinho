MIP for Sellers — rep card

The sentence

You manage their IT. Now you manage their intelligence.

A Managed Intelligence Provider goes beyond infrastructure support and ticket resolution. Instead of software, you deploy intelligence and manage outcomes.

The three drivers

Every MIP conversation reduces risk, buys back time, or grows the business. If it does none of those, it isn’t a MIP conversation.

The four levels — hear them, don’t define them

  • Chatbots — individuals prompting tools. → Education first.
  • Assistants — AI inside a workflow, suggesting. → Agent Store.
  • Digital Labor — work completed end to end. → Build offers.
  • Digital Workforce — multiple functions running. → Outcome pricing. Bring help.

The two questions that place anyone

  • “Does anyone in your leadership team own AI as a priority — or is it still left to individual technicians?”
  • “Have you sold any AI service to a client, even once? What did you charge?”

Early Discovery (no owner, no sales) — education first, don’t lead with product.
Exploring (champion or pilots, no revenue) — Agent Store bundles + a ProServ pilot.
Investing (named owner, budget, revenue) — deeper conversation, bring help.

One door, two routes

Agent Store is the entry point. From there: MIP Program (they build it) or Managed Intelligence Services (Pax8 ProServ delivers). “I like this but I don’t have the people” → that’s MIS.

Log it

Partner name · roughly where they are · what you offered · what you owe them. A conversation that isn’t logged didn’t happen.

The handoff rule

You don’t invent. You hand it to someone who knows, and you take their details.
