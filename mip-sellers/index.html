Module 2 · Why this, why now

What’s actually happening in your partners’ businesses

Before you learn to hear a partner, it’s worth knowing what they’re living through. None of this is AI for its own sake — it’s the commercial ground shifting under an MSP model that has worked for fifteen years.

01 · The shift has a name

The Agentic Inflection Point

Pax8’s thesis is that AI agents are moving from passive copilots that suggest to autonomous systems that execute. The projection behind it: a million-fold increase in effective AI capability over four years.

That’s the “why now”. Not that AI arrived — it arrived years ago — but that it stopped needing a human in the loop for every step.

02 · The pressure is commercial

Margin, not technology

30–50% projected decline in traditional MSP margins by 2027 for providers who don’t evolve

By 2026 the market splits in two: MSPs competing on price, and MIPs capturing disproportionate value. Your partners feel this before they can articulate it. When they say “we’re busy”, quite often they mean “we’re busy and it’s worth less than it used to be”.

03 · Your partners are already moving

Badly, and without a plan

~75% of SMBs are already using or experimenting with AI — almost none have a strategy

That gap is the entire opportunity. Not selling them AI — helping them put a shape around what their clients are already doing without them.

04 · What we hear from partners

“We’re not ready.” Not disinterest — fear of getting it wrong in front of their own clients.

“Which platform should we bet on?” Fear of backing the wrong horse publicly.

“How would we even charge for this?” They can picture spending. They can’t picture getting it back.

Module 3 · The concepts

Four words you need to understand, not just recognise

You’re about to place partners by what they say. That only works if these four mean something precise to you — because partners use them loosely, and often overstate where they are.

1 · Chatbots A person types a prompt and gets an answer back. The human decides what to ask, judges the answer, and does something with it. Copilot drafting an email is this. The value is real but personal — and invisible on a P&L.
2 · Assistants AI sits inside a workflow and does one useful thing without being asked each time — summarising every ticket as it lands. The human still decides and still acts; a step has been removed, not a job. First rung where it shows up in how the business runs.
3 · Digital Labor Work gets completed end to end rather than helped along. An agent closes a Level 1 ticket and writes the outcome back into the PSA. Nobody reviews each one. This is where it stops being a tool and starts being capacity.
4 · Digital Workforce Several functions running that way at once — client onboarding, billing reconciliation, security monitoring. The partner is no longer selling hours. At this point pricing has to change, because effort and value have decoupled.

Why you need this before you talk to partners

Hearing the rung is the qualification

Each rung has a different next step — education, Agent Store, building offers, outcome pricing. Get the rung wrong and you pitch the wrong thing: product to someone who needs education, or education to someone who’s ready to buy.

Partners also inflate. Someone with fifteen Copilot licences will say “we’re doing AI”. The words they use about the work — not about the tools — tell you the truth.

Where Copilot fits

The on-ramp most partners already own

Microsoft Copilot is the reason “we tried AI and it didn’t stick” is the single most common opening you’ll hear. It’s a rung-one experience being judged as if it were the whole ladder — which is why the reframe you’ll practise next isn’t “try a different tool”, it’s “pair it with one workflow”.
