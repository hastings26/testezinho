# "Nib & the Desk That Thinks" — MIP Hybrid Teaser
### Mini-production package · 30-second live-action + 2D hand-drawn teaser

> **Term check:** the correct term is **MIP — Managed Intelligence Provider** (Pax8's name for
> the next step after the MSP, the Managed Service Provider). It is *not* "MIB". Everything below uses MIP.

---

## A) OVERALL CREATIVE DIRECTION

### 1. Creative concept — "Nib & the Desk That Thinks"

Morning at a real IT partner's desk. Sticky notes, a laptop full of alerts, a router blinking,
licence renewals piling up. It's the everyday reality of an MSP: *managing things*.

From the margin of an open notebook, a small hand-drawn character called **Nib** peels itself
off the paper. Nib is a pencil-and-ink sketch come to life, curious and clever and a little
cheeky. Nib looks at the clutter, thinks, then picks up a real pencil (twice its height) and
**draws three tiny helper agents** onto the desk. The agents go to work: sorting tickets,
gathering the scattered notes, putting a small shield by the router. Then Nib uses the
pencil as a baton and **draws ink lines between the real objects**, turning a messy desk into
one connected, calm system.

The desk doesn't lose its humans. A real hand comes in, picks up the coffee, and the connected
lines turn into a single drawn card: **"Outcomes, not licences."**

**The metaphor in one line:** *an MSP manages the tools on the desk; an MIP draws the
intelligence that connects them.* The 2D character is the "intelligence" layer. The real
world is the business it serves. Neither replaces the other. They work together, and the
mix of media *is* the message.

**Tagline options**
- *From managing services to managing intelligence.* (primary)
- *Same desk. New intelligence.*
- *Don't just manage the tools. Orchestrate the intelligence.*

**Tone guardrail:** aspirational but honest. The teaser sells the *shift*, not specific product
features or statistics, so it stays accurate however the programme evolves.

### 2. Visual style summary

**Photoreal tabletop macro cinematography meets flat, hand-drawn 2D animation.**
Think a premium product commercial shot on a real desk with shallow depth of field and warm
morning window light, with a sketchbook character animated on top. The character is flat,
line-boiling pencil and ink with a single teal watercolour accent, and it sits *in* the space:
it casts soft real shadows and pushes real objects around, but it never becomes 3D.

### 3. Style bible

#### 3.1 What stays PHOTOREALISTIC (live action)
- The whole environment: wooden or oak-veneer desk, laptop, ceramic coffee mug with steam,
  yellow sticky notes, a small Wi-Fi router with LEDs, a phone, cables, a leather or linen
  notebook, a pencil, reading glasses, a small plant, window light.
- Human presence: hands, forearms, an out-of-focus shoulder. **Faces are never sharp**, so
  the audience can put themselves in the chair.
- All physics and lighting: real shadows, real reflections, real bokeh, real steam, real dust
  motes in the light.
- Screens show soft, blurred, generic UI glow. No legible real software, no third-party logos.

#### 3.2 What stays ILLUSTRATED (flat 2D, hand-drawn)
- **Nib**, the hero character.
- **The three helper agents** ("the Sprites"): tiny folded-paper shapes with dot eyes.
- **Ink "!" alerts**, drawn ticket cards, the **connection lines**, the small shield, and the
  final **outcome card** and **end-card lettering**.
- Rendering rules: visible graphite grain, ink line with slight wobble and taper, off-white
  paper fill with paper texture, **line boil** (lines redrawn slightly every frame, animated
  on 2s/12 fps), cross-hatch shading only, **no gradients, no 3D shading, no specular
  highlights, no subsurface skin**.
- Colour: graphite grey + sepia-black ink + warm off-white paper. **One accent colour only:
  teal (#00D09C)**, as a loose watercolour wash on Nib's scarf, the agents' eyes and the
  connection lines. A touch of blue (#1D4ED8) is allowed *only* on the end-card logo.

#### 3.3 Character sheet — Nib
- About 7 cm tall in the world (roughly the height of a coffee mug's handle).
- Soft bean-shaped body, oversized round head, two big ink-dot eyes with white catchlights
  drawn as blank paper, expressive eyebrows made of single pencil strokes.
- One curly cowlick drawn as a loose spiral (it bounces with every move).
- A short teal watercolour scarf. That's the signature silhouette element.
- Tiny rubber-eraser "shoes" (pink-beige, flat colour, hatch-shaded).
- Personality: **friendly, sharp, slightly playful.** Nib tilts its head before it acts
  (thinking), does small showman flourishes (playful), and never panics (confident).
- Acting reference: a bright junior consultant who's secretly the smartest person in the
  room, and nice about it.

#### 3.4 How illustrated elements interact with the real world
1. **Contact shadows are real.** Every 2D element casts a soft, photoreal shadow on the desk
   that matches the window light direction (key light from frame-left).
2. **Occlusion is real.** Nib can walk behind the mug and be hidden by it, and can be
   reflected (softly, still flat) on the laptop's glossy bezel.
3. **Cause and effect is real.** When Nib pushes a sticky note, the *real* note slides. When an
   agent hops on the keyboard, a *real* key dips. When Nib draws on the desk, the ink line
   sits flat on the wood grain like marker on a surface, following the perspective of the desk.
4. **Light touches the drawing, not the reverse.** Warm window light can tint Nib's paper fill
   slightly warmer, but Nib never gets rim-lit 3D shading. Nib's teal accents may give off
   a *faint* real glow onto the wood in the orchestration beat only.
5. **Scale is honest.** Nib's world is macro. The camera lives at desk height, so a pencil
   is a log and a mug is a building.

#### 3.5 Camera language
- **Macro tabletop cinematography**: 50–100 mm macro look, shallow depth of field (f/2–f/4),
  camera mostly at or just above desk height (Nib's eye line).
- Movement is **motion-controlled and deliberate**: slow push-ins, gentle lateral slides,
  one top-down crane, one slow orbit. No handheld shake. The drawing is the energetic part;
  the camera stays composed.
- Rack focus is the transition tool: from real object to Nib, and from Nib to the outcome.
- 16:9 master at 24 fps (live action). The 2D layer animates at 12 fps ("on 2s") inside it.
  Safe-frame the centre 9:16 for a vertical social cut.

#### 3.6 Lighting
- **Golden-hour morning window light** from frame-left, 3200–4000 K warm key, soft
  bounce fill. Gentle haze and dust motes in the beam.
- Practicals: laptop screen (cool, soft), router LEDs (small green/teal points), mug steam
  backlit.
- Arc: **cool and cluttered → warm and connected.** Shots 1–3 get slightly cooler with more
  screen spill. Shots 6–8 are warmer, and the light widens as the "system" connects.

#### 3.7 Pacing
- 30 seconds, 8 shots, average ~3.75 s per shot.
- Rhythm: **calm open (0–6 s) → tension (6–9 s) → build (9–22 s) → release (22–26 s) → sign-off (26–30 s).**
- The music (light pizzicato + soft piano + pencil-scratch percussion) starts sparse,
  adds layers as each agent appears, and resolves on a warm major chord at the outcome card.

#### 3.8 Mood
Warm, smart, modern, charming. **"Smart delight"**: the feeling of watching a clever
colleague make a hard thing look easy. Business-credible (real desk, real work, calm camera)
with a sprinkle of magic (the drawing). Never childish, never sci-fi, no robots, no
glowing-brain clichés, no blue-hologram AI tropes.

## Production notes

**Recommended workflow**
1. **Lock Nib first.** Generate the character sheet and pick one design. Use it as the
   character/style reference in every keyframe (e.g. Midjourney `--cref`/`--sref`, or a
   reference image in your image model of choice).
2. **Generate keyframes** for all 8 shots with *master prompt + shot prompt*. Review them side
   by side for consistent light direction, desk props and character.
3. **Animate image-to-video** (e.g. Runway, Kling, Veo, Luma) with the shot's video prompt.
   Keep clips short (3–5 s). Re-roll anything where Nib turns 3D or the desk turns cartoon.
4. **Most reliable route (if the AI video keeps "melting" the 2D style):** generate the desk
   as clean live-action plates *without* Nib, generate or animate Nib separately on a flat
   background (or have an illustrator draw 12 fps loops), then **composite** with a soft
   shadow pass. This fully protects the mixed-media contrast.
5. **Text in post, always.** Outcome card and end card are added in the edit, never
   generated.
6. **Sound:** light pizzicato strings + felt piano + pencil-scratch/paper foley. Resolve on a
   warm major chord at 0:22. A soft "tick" on each ink line connection.

**Deliverables:** 16:9 master (30 s) · 9:16 social cut (reframe centre, same timings) ·
6 s bumper (shots 6 → 8) · Shot 8 still as thumbnail/event slide.

**Accuracy guardrail:** the teaser communicates the MSP → MIP *shift* only. It makes no
claims about specific products, availability dates or performance stats. Before external
use, check the end-card wording and logo lockup against current approved MIP messaging and
brand guidelines.
